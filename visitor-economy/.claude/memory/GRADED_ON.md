# What destination marketing professionals are actually graded on

Research pass 2026-08-05. This is the doc that should drive both the index
rubric and any outreach copy. Sources at the bottom.

The short version: **the metrics a DMO marketing coordinator personally
controls are exactly the metrics their board has stopped accepting.** That gap
is the entire opening.

---

## The grading stack

A county tourism office is graded at three levels, by three different
audiences, on three different things. Anyone writing to a marketing
coordinator is writing to layer 3 about a layer 1 problem.

### Layer 1 — Does this agency deserve its budget? (county commissioners)

In Pennsylvania a county tourism promotion agency is not a normal nonprofit.
It is a **statutory creature funded by hotel room rental tax**. Under 16
Pa.C.S. § 17507 the county *must* pass all of that tax revenue to the
recognised tourist promotion agency within 60 days, and the agency *must*
file an audited annual report with the county commissioners within 90 days
of its fiscal year end. Funds may only be spent "marketing the area served
by the agency as a leisure travel destination or as a business, convention
or meeting travel destination."

So layer 1 is not a KPI dashboard. It is a legal filing and a political
question: **is this money doing anything visible?**

Metrics that live here: hotel tax revenue collected, room nights, occupancy,
the audited annual report itself.

### Layer 2 — Is the marketing working? (the board / executive director)

Destinations International, the industry body, now draws a hard line between
*direct* and *indirect* indicators, and tells DMOs to report the direct ones:

| Direct (what boards now want) | Indirect (the legacy dashboard) |
|---|---|
| Room tax revenue | Click-through rate |
| Lodging metrics, occupancy | Impressions |
| Number of trips | Website traffic |
| Visitor spending | Cost per click |
| Return on ad spend | |
| Attribution | |

The shift is fast and recent. The share of DMOs focused primarily on
*awareness* dropped from 59% to 25% in a single year. Roughly 72% now
prioritise economic-impact data.

### Layer 3 — What the coordinator personally produces (their actual job)

The marketing director / social media coordinator — which per the Jul 21
session is exactly who Relay targets, deliberately not the CMO — is measured
on what they can produce and report:

- Earned media value (EMV)
- Content volume and asset library growth
- Social engagement, follower growth, reach
- Website sessions, campaign performance
- Partner and stakeholder satisfaction

**Every single one of these is on the "indirect" side of the table above.**

## The squeeze, stated plainly

The coordinator is hired to produce reach. The board has stopped counting
reach. Nobody has told the coordinator what to produce instead, and the
things that would satisfy the board — room nights, attributed visits — are
not things a social media coordinator can move directly or measure with the
tools they have.

The trade press describes exactly this failure mode: a mid-sized coastal
tourism board ran influencer campaigns for three years with macro creators
(500K–2M followers), produced good-looking content, and when the board asked
for proof of visitor impact the marketing team **had nothing credible to
offer**. Result: budget frozen, two fiscal cycles to demonstrate value or
lose the line item.

That is the nightmare scenario for a layer-3 person, and it is the thing to
write to.

## Why creator content is the bridge (the actual argument)

Creator work is the only layer-3 activity that produces layer-1 evidence:

1. **It has faces and names.** "Twelve people who live within an hour of here
   came, posted, and tagged us" is a sentence a commissioner understands. "4.2
   million impressions" is not.
2. **It is attributable by construction.** You know who went, when, and what
   they made, because you invited them.
3. **It produces a physical artifact for the annual report.** The statutory
   filing needs to show the money did something. A grid of owned content
   is that, and the office keeps it forever.
4. **The benchmark is defensible.** Destination influencer campaigns run
   roughly **$3–$7 earned media value per $1 spent**, top quartile above $10.
   That is a number a coordinator can put in a board deck and defend.
5. **Micro beats macro on exactly the axis boards now care about.** The
   coastal-board failure was a *macro* creator programme. Local micro-creators
   produce visits, not just impressions.

The pitch is therefore **not** "get more reach." It is: *here is something you
can show the people who decide your budget.*

Relay's already-drafted tourism value props line up with this and should be
kept — "Locals talking up your county. You don't manage a thing." / "Content
you keep. Forever, not just this season." / "We find the creators."

## Washington County PA — why it is the right case study

It is already the proof point in Relay's DMO prospecting sequence, already an
active account (creator Hannah Merkley has delivered), and its service
agreement is the template every other EP contract is modelled on.

But the reason it is a *good* case study is that layer 1 is on fire.

- The county levies a **5% hotel tax** worth about **$2.2M a year**. The
  Tourism Promotion Agency currently receives **96% of it, roughly $2.1M**.
- In December 2025 Commissioners **Nick Sherman** and **Electra Janis** moved
  to redraft the hotel tax ordinance so that **75% goes to a joint county
  fund** requiring mutual agreement, the agency keeps **20%**, and the
  treasurer takes **5%** as an administrative fee. The stated purpose is to
  fund a **sports recreation and convention centre**. Commissioner **Larry
  Maggi** opposed.
- Agency president **Jeff Kotula** said the plan "violates state law and could
  end up in litigation against the county," citing § 17507.
- This is not a one-off. Commissioners moved to **halt funding** to the agency
  in May 2025 and hired **special counsel to appeal a denial for tourism
  files** in August 2025. It is a running, year-plus fight over whether the
  agency should exist in its current form.

⚠ **Currency caveat:** the most recent reporting found is December 2025. No
2026 resolution, filed lawsuit, or redrafted ordinance was located. Treat the
outcome as **unresolved and unverified**, and confirm before any of this is
said out loud to the account. Do not tell Washington County what is happening
inside Washington County.

**What it means for the argument.** This is a tourism office whose own funders
are actively asking what the money buys, with a legal defence but a weak
evidentiary one. A statute tells them they are owed the money. It does not
tell a commissioner the money worked. That is precisely the gap creator
content fills, and it is why the case study should be framed around
*evidence*, not reach.

---

## How this should change the index

The current rubric grades an office on creator readiness from Relay's point of
view. That is useful internally and useless to the person reading it.

Reframe: every office page should also answer **"what could this office show
its board?"** Same five measured signals, read back as evidence-production
capacity rather than as vendor-readiness. An office with a media kit and an
asset library can already produce board-ready artifacts. An office with
neither is running on impressions.

That reframe costs nothing in data and changes who the page is for.
